## Design Context

### Users

Waleed's friends, blog visitors, and later community contributors arriving at LibXNDR. Curious readers who want to browse the founding collection, open books in a comfortable reader, find new sources with the finder, and keep a private reading life. Guests explore and read public PDFs; an account is required to save, run batch finds, annotate, contribute, or use LLM features. Reviewers get a separate source-focused room.

### Brand Personality

A cybernetic reading room with Egyptian memory. Serious, generous, a little mysterious at the edges but never cryptic in what it says. The terminal voice lives in the chrome (headings, labels, buttons, the `>` prompt); the sentences a reader actually reads are plain, direct, and readable.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Dark only.** Near-black space (`--bg #07070a`) with a faint scanline texture and a whisper of gold at the top. Display type is glowing bone-white VT323; labels, nav, buttons and tags are IBM Plex Mono; every sentence is IBM Plex Sans at 16px or larger with no glow.

**Book covers are the colour.** Real covers stay exactly as they are and are the brightest thing on any page; each tile and the preview glow faintly in that cover's own dominant colour.

**Egyptian symbolism as components, not decoration.** A fixed glyph set (`templates/partials/glyphs.svg`) of flat colour-block SVG hieroglyphs on a 24px grid — pyramid, wedjat eye, ibis, scroll, ankh, scarab, feather of Ma'at, cartouche, djed, sun, lotus, owl, reed, water. Each has a job (ibis = find, ankh = save, eye = preview, feather = review, lotus = my library). The app opens with a gold pyramid, carved with glyphs, spinning while the shelf loads; the same loader runs inline wherever the library is working. Palette: Egyptian pigments — gold, lapis, turquoise, carnelian, malachite — with turquoise and carnelian carrying the old teal/orange identity.

**Avoid:** light paper themes, generic SaaS dashboards, glassmorphism, rainbow neon, ASCII-only mockups, `//` comments or `[ ]` brackets in copy, monospace body text, walls of hieroglyphs, and any preview that dumps file metadata or empty notes at the reader.

### Quiet mode (since 2026-10-19)

The site now has strangers on it, so it speaks softly. Visitors see a calm hero, thirty curated covers ("A look inside"), and a sign-in prompt; search, A–Z, by-author, and reading are member features. Glow stays only on the logo, the count, and the letter headings; eyebrows are sentence case; display type is one size smaller. The web PDF finder is reached only through the blinking wedjat **eye** in the top bar and is explained once on the landing page; its page has no headline, just the eyebrow and one line: "This tool points you to a fair-use, viewable PDF of any book on the internet." Contributing appears only as "Send to Waleed" on a finder result. Never add a "Find books" nav item, a standalone contribute form, or legal wording about the finder.

### Design Principles

1. **The collection is the protagonist.** Lead with covers, titles and authors; the system frames them in black and glow.
2. **Keep the owner's copy.** Headlines and sentences on the live site are deliberate. Change wording only when asked.
3. **One main action per surface.** A book preview has Read (or Open source) and Save; for visitors it has only Sign in to read. Home has Browse A–Z for members and Sign in to read for visitors. Nothing else competes.
4. **A look inside for visitors, the library for members.** Visitors see thirty covers and a preview card; reading, searching, saving, the eye, and contributing all sit behind sign-in.
5. **Reading stays calm.** The PDF page keeps its own light; dark chrome stays out of the way; notes and the companion sit beside the text.
6. **Plain words inside terminal frames.** Mono uppercase only for tiny eyebrows and tags. Labels are words, not symbols.
7. **Build for a large corpus and slow phones.** Sticky A–Z rail, lazy covers, dialog previews that need no full page load, no frameworks, iOS Safari first.
